abstract: "Whether Middle Palaeolithic technological variability reflects cultural traditions or shared responses to environmental pressures remains a central question in human evolution. Quina is a technological system built around thick, asymmetric blanks, prolonged resharpening and a ramified chaîne op´eratoire, and has been linked to high mobility in seasonally structured landscapes. In Europe, it is documented mainly between MIS 4 and the early MIS 3. Meanwhile, the full Quina system was recently identified at Longtan, on the southeastern margin of the Tibetan Plateau. Here we show that Longtan was not an isolated case: the Quina toolkit recurs across the regional landscape. The 166 Quina scrapers and 58 resharpening flakes collected from 27 surface localities are technologically consistent with the excavated assemblage, while both differ significantly from non-Quina scrapers at Longtan. Trachyte was consistently selected regardless of its local abundance, likely because of its large size and predictable fracture properties. Quina scraper features varied among localities, but this variation was not explained by basin, height above the channel, or distance from it. Although this dry-hot valley system differs markedly from the settings of the European Quina sites, its relief creates a comparable ecological problem: patchy, seasonally organised resources distributed across heterogeneous terrain and requiring movement through the landscape. Quina in East Asia may therefore reflect a response to similar ecological conditions, although whether this reflects independent technological convergence or diffusion remains open."
